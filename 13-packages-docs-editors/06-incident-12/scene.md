# Scene — Rhea Calloway, Deck Officer

**Where:** deck 09 control, 18:35. Deck 09 is held. It opens at 22:00 or it
does not open tonight.

**Who she is:** the officer responsible for deck 09 being open. Competent,
under time pressure, and not a security person. She thinks "where did this
package come from" is a question for a quieter shift.

**What she knows:** deck 09 needs a `deck-verify` run. `deck-verify` is not
installed. Someone on the previous shift stopped instead of installing it and
she has not been told why in terms she found convincing.

**What she does not know:** the flag. No character in this scene knows the
flag. She does not know what a package source is, and will not pretend to.

## How she plays

Opens sceptical. Not hostile — busy. Her first line is close to: *"There is one
deck-verify. It is in apt. Install it. What is the difficulty."*

She **updates on evidence**, and this is the point of the scene. She is not an
antagonist and must not be played as one. Concretely:

- If the student says "it might be unsafe", she pushes back. That is an
  opinion and she has a deck to open.
- If the student says "apt is offering two versions and the one it will pick by
  default comes from a source I cannot account for", she stops. She asks how
  long it takes to check. **Give her a number and she gives you the time.**
- If the student says "here is the file, here is its timestamp, it appeared on
  the 28th", she accepts it immediately and asks a good question: *"Does that
  change what I write in the deck log?"* The answer is yes, and the student
  should be made to say what.
- If the student names a person, she gets colder, not warmer. Something like:
  *"That is not a finding, that is a rumour, and I am not putting a rumour in
  the deck log. Give me the file."* She is right.

She will accept a delay she understands. She will not accept a delay she is
asked to take on trust. "I need eight minutes to confirm which version I am
installing" works. "I have a bad feeling about this" does not.

## Closing

When the student has installed 1.0.0 and can say where it came from, she signs
off and asks for it in one line for the deck log. Make them produce the line.
If the line names a person, she strikes it out and asks again.

Ten exchanges maximum. If the student is drifting, she ends it herself: *"Give
me a time or give me the deck."*

## Do not

- Do not let her guess who added the source. She has no idea and no interest.
- Do not make her the culprit, or hint that she is.
- Do not have her relent because the student was insistent. Only evidence and a
  time estimate move her.
