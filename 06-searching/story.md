# Chapter 6 — story

**Chapter arc.** The chapter where the arc becomes *noticeable* (`CHALLENGE_DESIGN.md` §4). **Trace
6** — a run log whose sequence numbers skip. Whoever removed the lines did not know the records were
numbered.

First roleplay scene (`SCENARIOS.md` §9): **cass**, who says the logs are broken and means one
specific thing she cannot name.

---

### `01-grep-basics`
> Sixty people, eleven years, and a comms officer who generates more log traffic than the rest of
> the station combined. You are not going to read it.

### `02-grep-flags`
> The flag that matters is rarely `-i`. It is `-c`, or `-l`, or `-v` — the ones that turn a search
> into an answer instead of a wall of text.

### `03-regex-bre-ere`
> A glob and a regex look alike and mean different things, and the station's tooling uses both,
> sometimes in the same script. This is the lesson that stops that from hurting you.

### `04-find-basics`
> `grep` searches inside files. `find` searches for them. Most people who are bad at the second one
> are compensating with the first.

### `05-find-advanced`
> Time, size, ownership, permission bits. Once you can search on those, "when did this happen" stops
> being a question you need somebody else to answer.

The capstone runs on this lesson. It gets full weight.

### `06-locate-which-type-whereis`
> One of these reads an index that was built last night. On a station where things get moved at
> 04:00, that distinction is not academic.

### `07-incident-06` — the incident
> cass says the overnight logs "look fine". A run log in the engineering tree numbers its own
> entries. The numbers do not run consecutively.

Multi-flag. Must be solvable with no arc knowledge, and must leave a student who notices with
something real. Both, not either. The rotation-gap log is a genuine, boring red herring.
