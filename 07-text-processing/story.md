# Chapter 7 — story

**Chapter arc.** **Trace 7** — `eng-svc`, an account with one login fourteen months ago that appears
in no passwd file. It predates dorn noticing anything, so the student meets the adjustment's
fingerprint before they meet the investigation.

Roleplay: **the captain**, who wants "the access numbers" and will not specify a format.

---

### `01-wc-sort-uniq`
> Three commands and one idiom that between them answer most of the questions anybody on this
> station will ever ask you about a log file.

### `02-cut-and-paste`
> The station's logs are columns. Everything downstream gets easier the moment you can take one.

### `03-tr`
> The bluntest tool in the chapter and the right one about once a week.

### `04-sed-substitution`
> You will be handed files that are almost the right shape. This is how you fix ten thousand lines
> without opening any of them.

### `05-awk-fields`
> `awk` is a programming language pretending to be a command. You need about six percent of it, and
> this is that six percent.

### `06-tee-and-xargs`
> Reports go two places: the person who asked, and the file you will need when they ask again.

### `07-building-a-pipeline`
> Nobody writes a nine-stage pipeline. They write one stage, look at it, and add another. The people
> who look fast at this are just people who never guessed.

The method lesson. The capstone's forensics work is this lesson repeated under pressure.

### `08-incident-07` — the incident
> The captain wants the access log as a ranked report by account. Top talkers, counts, readable. By
> the end of the shift.

The report is the exercise; the finding is in its tail. `ops-bot` dominates the top so completely
that students stop reading — the answer is a count of exactly one, at the bottom.
