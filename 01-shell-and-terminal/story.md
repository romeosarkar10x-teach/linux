# Chapter 1 — story

**Chapter arc.** The student's first contact with dorn's leftovers — a shell history that stops in
the middle of a word. At this point it reads as a disconnected session, nothing more.
**Trace 1** (`SCENARIOS.md` §4). Nothing here may suggest concealment.

---

### `01-terminal-vs-shell-vs-tty`
> Four decks, sixty-odd people, and a rack of machines that have been up for eleven years. You will
> be talking to them through a window that is not the thing it talks to. Worth knowing which is
> which before you start giving orders.

### `02-shells-on-the-box`
> The station's accounts do not agree on what a shell is. Some of that is history. Some of it is
> somebody in a hurry, eleven years ago, and it is still here.

### `03-command-anatomy`
> Half of what you type is not the program you think it is. On a machine with eleven years of
> accumulated helpfulness bolted to it, that is worth checking before it matters.

### `04-variables`
> Everything the station's tooling knows about you, it knows because something set a variable.
> Including the things that are wrong.

### `05-readline`
> You will type roughly forty thousand commands on this posting. The people who are fast at this are
> not fast typists — they are people who stopped retyping things.

### `06-history`
> Every account on the station keeps a record of what it typed. Yours will too. This is the lesson
> where that stops being trivia.

### `07-incident-01` — the incident
> Your predecessor's account still has a shell history. The last line in it stops in the middle of
> a word. Three weeks, and nobody has looked.

Full 5-beat structure and constraint in `SCENARIOS.md` §7. The history file is read-only by rule;
the validator checks it is unmodified.
