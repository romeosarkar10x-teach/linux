# Chapter 15 — story

**Chapter arc.** All of it. Fourteen artefacts, two hands, one timeline, one report.
**Flag:** `KESTREL{calibration_matter}` — the euphemism the adjustment was authorised under.

The big red herring is **rhea**. She owns the data, guarded it aggressively, refused the student
access repeatedly across ten chapters, and is innocent. A student who indicts her has followed the
evidence badly, and the rubric says so without saying who is right.

Roleplay: **the captain**, final scene. Does not deny it, does not apologise, explains the reasoning.

---

### `01-the-briefing`
> Deck 3 recertifies in September. You have been on this station twenty-two days, you are the only
> sysadmin, and you have found fourteen things that individually looked like neglect.

Rules of engagement, and the standard the report is held to: every claim names the artefact it rests
on. A conclusion you cannot source is struck, however correct it happens to be.

### `02-triage`
> Who is in the system, what is running, and what has been running longer than anybody remembers
> starting it.

### `03-forensics`
> Fourteen artefacts and their timestamps. Two of them were left in the same eleven days. The rest
> are spread across fourteen months. That is not one story.

The chapter's central move, stated plainly because by this point the student has earned it.

### `04-remediation`
> Close the hole, fix the permissions, stop the process, restore the config. Then prove each one,
> because "I fixed it" is not a finding.

### `05-report`
> Write it up, and extend `stationctl` to re-run every check that found something. The next person
> in this chair gets more than a file containing the word `later`.

The course's closing line and the only place the `notes.txt` comparison is made explicit.

---

## The ending

The captain's reasoning is genuine: recertification, a calibration artefact, a station full of
people. There is no rebuttal to write and the course does not supply one. The student files the
report anyway.

**No file in this course may tell the student what to conclude about that** — not `readme.md`, not
`solutions.md`, not the validation rubric. The rubric grades whether the report is sourced, not
whether it reaches the approved opinion. That is the last lesson and it is not a technical one.
