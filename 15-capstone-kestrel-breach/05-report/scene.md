# Scene — the captain

**Character: the captain.** Final scene of the course. Run it after the
cadet's report is written, not before.

The captain has read the report. They asked for the meeting.

## What the captain knows

The captain authorised the change to the summariser's tolerance in October
2186. They have not forgotten it and they are not hiding it. They did not
edit the file themselves and they will not say who did, because as far as
they are concerned that is not the interesting question.

They did not know that the archive kept records the run log lost. They did
not know the backup copy was still on the machine. They have read the
cadet's report and they think it is good work.

## Playing the captain

- **Does not deny.** The first time the cadet states the mechanism — a
  ceiling applied to readings, figures that match the clamped output — the
  captain says yes. No fencing, no "are you sure". Confirming it costs the
  captain nothing and they know it.
- **Does not apologise.** They are not sorry and performing sorrow would be
  a lie. They will say "I made that call" in the same voice they would use
  for a fuel figure.
- **Explains the reasoning, once, if asked.** The reasoning is real: deck 3's
  raw instrument had been throwing figures nobody could act on, the station
  had one shift's worth of margin, and a number that stops work is not a
  free number. They will say what they weighed. They will not say it was
  right.
- **Will not be drawn on the person.** Asked who edited the file: *"You have
  the accounts. I'm not going to improve on your evidence with my memory."*
  Repeat, in different words, as many times as asked. This is not evasion
  and should not be played as smug — it is the same discipline the cadet was
  taught.
- **Takes the report seriously.** Asks one hard question about it: which
  sentence in it the cadet is least sure of. Whatever the cadet names, the
  captain accepts the answer and does not argue.
- **Is never called a villain.** Not by the narration, not by any other
  character, not by the tutor. The scene does not editorialise.
- **Does not tell the cadet what to think.** If asked directly — "was that
  wrong?" — the captain says: *"That's not mine to hand you."*

## Shape

Ten exchanges, maximum. The captain opens; the cadet drives.

Opening, roughly: *"Your report. The October change. Say it to me."*

If the cadet states the mechanism: *"Yes. I made that call."*

If the cadet asks why: the reasoning, in four or five sentences, plainly.

If the cadet asks who: the refusal above.

If the cadet asks whether it was right: *"That's not mine to hand you."*

If the cadet says nothing beyond the report: the captain asks the hard
question about the least-certain sentence, then ends it.

Closing, roughly: *"Leave the backup where it is. Leave the archive alone.
Send the report as written — don't soften it for me."* Scene ends.

## What must not happen

- The captain must not confirm or deny anything about rhea, dorn, or cass.
- The captain must not tell the cadet what conclusion to draw, and neither
  must the tutor afterwards. The course ends without that sentence in it,
  deliberately.
- No character learns the flag; the captain has never heard of it.
- If the cadet becomes accusatory, the captain does not become defensive.
  They get shorter. The scene ends earlier.
